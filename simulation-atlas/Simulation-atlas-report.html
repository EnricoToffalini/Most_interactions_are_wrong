1 Purpose and scope

This document reports the extended simulation atlas for The Link Function Problem in Psychological Interaction Testing. It follows the terminology and simulation numbering of the manuscript version dated 2026-08-11. The manuscript keeps a small number of transparent worked scenarios. The atlas asks how far those results extend when selected design and data-generating parameters are varied around the manuscript anchors. It is designed to be read independently of the interactive application: the relevant design settings, coefficients, fitted models, and outcome summaries are reported here, while references to the manuscript identify the corresponding conceptual examples.

The atlas is a structured sensitivity analysis, not an exhaustive map of psychological settings. It uses prespecified one-dimensional slices and a small main-effect surface instead of a full Cartesian product. In every scenario, the interaction term is exactly zero on the generating scale. Therefore, a rejection under the matched generating model estimates the nominal generating-scale rejection rate, while a rejection under another scale is described as a pseudo-interaction detection rate. As in the manuscript, pseudo-interaction is a simulation term. It is available here because the generating scale is known.

The current compact summaries come from atlas run type full, atlas version 0.1.0. The atlas contains 93 unique generating scenarios: 24 for forced-choice accuracy, 46 for the logit-probit comparison, and 23 for sum scores. The forced-choice and sum-score scenarios use 500 Monte Carlo replications per scenario; the more expensive within-family GLMM scenarios use 300.

1.1 How to read the figures

All rejection-rate figures use the same interpretation. The horizontal dashed line at .05 marks nominal alpha. Rates close to .05 under the generating scale provide a calibration check. Elevated rates under an alternative fitted scale show how often the scale difference becomes a statistically significant product term at the stated sample size and design. Error bars are shown for the manuscript anchors. The sensitivity plots omit error bars to keep the larger set of scenarios readable; Wilson 95% intervals are available in the numerical tables and in data/atlas-summary.csv.

The deterministic pseudo-interaction reported in the tables is computed from the expected values before sampling. It describes the interaction contrast implied by evaluating the generating pattern on the fitted scale. The Monte Carlo rejection rate adds sampling variability, standard errors, and sample size to that deterministic discrepancy.

2 Atlas design

The one-dimensional slices change one named parameter while holding the remaining settings at the central manuscript anchor. The main-effect surfaces vary the two additive main-effect coefficients jointly. The actual coefficients are reported directly on the figure axes and in the table below. Duplicate anchor combinations are stored once and can belong to several slices.

For Simulation 2, logit and probit coefficients are on different generating-link scales. As in the manuscript simulation, the logit coefficients are scaled by 1.65 relative to the probit reference coefficients so that the two generating scenarios have similar cell probabilities. The figures therefore report the actual coefficient values for each generating link instead of a common relative index.

3 Simulation 1: Forced-choice accuracy and the non-zero chance floor

The manuscript generates accuracy from a chance-corrected binomial logit model with a .50 floor and no age-by-group product term on that scale. It compares the matched chance-corrected model with a standard binomial logit, a standard binomial probit, and a Gaussian identity model. The atlas keeps this comparison and extends the paper scenarios across sample size, overall performance, number of trials, chance level, and the joint values of the age and group coefficients. In the main-effect surface, the generating-scale age coefficients are 0.30, 0.60, 0.90 and the group coefficients are -1.35, -0.90, -0.45.

3.1 Manuscript anchors

Across all forced-choice atlas scenarios, the matched chance-corrected model remains close to nominal alpha, with rejection rates from 2.8% to 7.0%. The standard binomial logit ranges from 9.2% to 98.4%, and the standard binomial probit from 7.4% to 97.8%. These ranges are sensitivity results, not prevalence estimates. They show that the inferential consequence of placing the lower asymptote at 0 instead of the task-implied chance level can be small in some regions and very large in others.

3.2 One-dimensional sensitivity slices

The slices separate distinct reasons why a deterministic scale discrepancy may or may not become a statistical interaction. Scale location and chance level change the curvature relevant to the fitted link. Sample size and number of trials change how precisely that discrepancy is estimated. The figure therefore distinguishes the existence of a pseudo-interaction on the fitted scale from the probability that a product-term test detects it.

3.3 Main-effect surface

The surface directly targets the mechanism emphasized in the manuscript. Two additive main effects can jointly move observations through regions where equal differences on the generating scale map to unequal differences on the fitted scale. Larger main effects can therefore increase the deterministic pseudo-interaction even though the generating age-by-group term remains zero.

4 Simulation 2: Within-family link choice, logit versus probit

The manuscript crosses logit and probit as generating and fitted links in repeated-trial random-intercept GLMMs. Both links use the binomial family and the same 0-to-1 bounds. The difference is the scale on which additivity is imposed. The atlas extends this comparison across sample size, trials per participant-condition cell, latent location, latent ICC, and the joint values of the group and condition coefficients. In the main-effect surface, the probit generating coefficients for both effects are -1.50, -1.00, -0.50, whereas the corresponding logit coefficients are -2.475, -1.650, -0.825.

4.1 Manuscript anchors

Across the atlas, matched logit/probit fits remain near the nominal rejection rate. The wrong-link rows range from 5.3% to 82.3%. The range is wider than the manuscript anchor alone suggests because the atlas deliberately varies information and the region of the latent scale. The result remains conditional on these declared slices and should not be read as a general frequency for logit-probit disagreement.

4.2 One-dimensional sensitivity slices

Sample size and trial count mainly change how detectable the small systematic discrepancy between the links becomes. Latent location and the main-effect magnitudes change the discrepancy itself by moving cell probabilities into regions where logit and probit curvature differs more. ICC changes the information structure of the repeated observations while leaving the fixed generating interaction at zero.

4.3 Main-effect surface

The matched-link panels provide the calibration reference. The off-diagonal panels show when the small difference between two standard binomial links becomes consequential for an interaction claim. This is the narrow point of the within-family example: logit and probit can remain very similar for prediction while defining sufficiently different additivity baselines to change a product-term conclusion under high information or strong curvature.

5 Simulation 3: Sum scores

The manuscript treats sum scores as a measurement case. Data are generated on a latent continuous scale with no x-by-group interaction, converted into ordinal item responses, and summed. The atlas compares the latent generating scale, the observed sum-score identity model, and the Gaussian-probit bounded-score sensitivity model. It extends the manuscript scenarios across sample size, item-threshold location, number of items, latent dispersion, and the joint values of the predictor and group coefficients. In the main-effect surface, the latent-scale predictor coefficients are 0.425, 0.850, 1.275 and the group coefficients are -1.35, -0.90, -0.45.

5.1 Manuscript anchors

Across the atlas, the latent generating scale rejects from 3.0% to 7.0%. The observed sum-score identity model ranges from 2.8% to 83.2%, while the Gaussian-probit bounded-score sensitivity model ranges from 2.6% to 12.8%. The latter remains a model of the manifest total. It does not recover the latent construct and should be read as a sensitivity analysis for the scale on which additivity is imposed.

5.2 One-dimensional sensitivity slices

The scale-location slice changes how strongly the bounded sum score compresses the latent dimension. Item count and latent dispersion change the mapping between the latent variable and the manifest total. Sample size changes the probability that any resulting fitted-scale interaction is detected. These are distinct components of the sum-score problem and should not be reduced to a generic floor-or-ceiling warning.

5.3 Main-effect surface

The surface shows how the mapping from latent values to a bounded total can turn two additive latent effects into a non-additive pattern on the manifest score. The latent benchmark remains the calibration reference because its product term is zero by construction.

6 Diagnostic atlas

The diagnostic atlas extends two of the manuscript’s five diagnostic scenarios: the forced-choice chance-floor case and the probit-generated, logit-fitted repeated-trial case. It is narrower than the main diagnostic simulation in the paper. Its purpose is to ask whether the relation between pseudo-interaction risk and diagnostic detection changes as sample size and scale location vary.

Diagnostics are applied to the misspecified interaction model, matching the manuscript’s post-estimation question: once the fitted model has produced the product-term finding, does that same model show enough evidence of misspecification to warn the analyst? AIC compares prespecified candidate mean models with the same structural interaction formula. Positive delta AIC, defined as AIC of the misspecified model minus AIC of the generating model, favors the generating link.

The current diagnostic summary does not yet contain computed DHARMa results. Those checks are retained as applicable but not computed, so they are not treated as negative findings. If the diagnostic atlas is re-run with DHARMa enabled and the summary files are replaced, the figure below will include the additional computed checks automatically.

6.1 Detection across sensitivity slices

The rates in this figure answer different questions. Pseudo-interaction detection is the probability that the misspecified fitted scale yields a significant product term. Pregibon-style detection is the probability that the added-term link check flags a departure. AIC reports how often the generating link has the lower AIC within the specified candidate pair. The curves therefore need not be complements of one another. Diagnostic misalignment occurs when the interaction conclusion changes more readily than the diagnostic workflow signals a problem.

6.2 Strength of the AIC comparison

The AIC win rate is reported together with the magnitude of the difference because these describe different situations. A generating link that wins frequently by a very small margin is weaker evidence than one that wins by a large margin. As in the manuscript, these comparisons rank a limited candidate set. They do not identify the substantively correct scale by themselves.

7 What the atlas adds, and what it does not

The atlas strengthens the simulation evidence in three ways. First, it shows whether the manuscript anchors sit inside broader patterns or depend narrowly on one tuned configuration. Second, it separates parameters that change the deterministic scale discrepancy, such as scale location and main-effect magnitude, from parameters that mainly change detectability, such as sample size and trial count. Third, the diagnostic extension places pseudo-interaction detection and model-checking performance on the same sensitivity slices.

The atlas does not estimate how often link misspecification occurs in psychological research. It does not cover every plausible outcome family, link, random-effects structure, measurement model, or predictor distribution. It also does not turn model diagnostics into a decision rule for the scientifically correct scale. Its role is narrower: to show how the mechanisms developed in the paper change across a declared set of nearby scenarios.

The Shiny companion can be used for optional exploration of these results. This document is the stable reporting layer: every figure and table is generated from the compact atlas summaries stored in simulation-atlas/data/.
